# <span style={{ fontFamily: '"Times New Roman", Times, serif' }}><span style={{ color: "#059669" }}>Grammar & Mechanics Checklist</span></span>

<span style={{ color: "#059669", fontWeight: 700 }}>Verbal Reasoning (Text Completion, Sentence Equivalence, Reading Comprehension) never tests grammar directly — but Analytical Writing does.</span> Here's what graders actually pay attention to when <span style={{ color: "#7c3aed", fontWeight: 700 }}>evaluating sentence-level control in your essay.</span>

## <span style={{ fontFamily: '"Times New Roman", Times, serif' }}>1. Sentence Structure & Variety</span>
- Avoid run-on sentences and fragments.
- <span style={{ color: "#b45309", fontWeight: 700 }}>Mix simple, compound, and complex sentences</span> — not just short choppy ones or one long tangled one.
- <span style={{ color: "#7c3aed", fontWeight: 700 }}>Use subordinate clauses (*although, because, while*, etc.) correctly to show logical relationships.</span>

## <span style={{ fontFamily: '"Times New Roman", Times, serif' }}>2. Subject-Verb Agreement</span>
- Watch for cases where the <span style={{ color: "#b45309", fontWeight: 700 }}>subject and verb are separated by a clause.</span>
- Example: "The *benefits* of the policy, despite its cost, *remain* significant" (not "remains").

## <span style={{ fontFamily: '"Times New Roman", Times, serif' }}>3. Verb Tense Consistency</span>
- <span style={{ color: "#7c3aed", fontWeight: 700 }}>Don't shift between past and present tense without reason within the same argument.</span>

## <span style={{ fontFamily: '"Times New Roman", Times, serif' }}>4. Pronoun Clarity</span>
- <span style={{ color: "#b45309", fontWeight: 700 }}>Every "it," "this," "they" should have one obvious antecedent.</span>
- <span style={{ color: "#7c3aed", fontWeight: 700 }}>Vague pronoun reference is one of the most common issues that costs clarity points.</span>

## <span style={{ fontFamily: '"Times New Roman", Times, serif' }}>5. Parallel Structure</span>
- <span style={{ color: "#b45309", fontWeight: 700 }}>Lists and comparisons should use matching grammatical forms.</span>
- Example: "The policy would reduce congestion, improve air quality, and *saving* costs" should be "*save* costs" — parallel with "reduce" and "improve."

## <span style={{ fontFamily: '"Times New Roman", Times, serif' }}>6. Punctuation</span>
- <span style={{ color: "#7c3aed", fontWeight: 700 }}>Avoid comma splices (joining two full sentences with just a comma).</span>
- <span style={{ color: "#b45309", fontWeight: 700 }}>Use semicolons correctly for closely related independent clauses.</span>
- <span style={{ color: "#7c3aed", fontWeight: 700 }}>Not essential to be perfect, but frequent errors here signal weak control.</span>

## <span style={{ fontFamily: '"Times New Roman", Times, serif' }}>7. Word Choice & Precision</span>
- Use words correctly, not just impressively (e.g., <span style={{ color: "#b45309", fontWeight: 700 }}>"affect" vs. "effect," "fewer" vs. "less"</span>).
- <span style={{ color: "#7c3aed", fontWeight: 700 }}>Overly fancy vocabulary used incorrectly hurts more than plain, correct vocabulary.</span>

## <span style={{ fontFamily: '"Times New Roman", Times, serif' }}>What Graders Are NOT Nitpicking</span>
- <span style={{ color: "#b45309", fontWeight: 700 }}>A few minor typos or occasional small errors won't tank your score.</span>
- This isn't a copyediting test — perfect academic-journal-level prose isn't the bar.

## <span style={{ fontFamily: '"Times New Roman", Times, serif' }}>The Real Bar</span>

<span style={{ color: "#059669", fontWeight: 700 }}>Your writing needs to be clear enough that a reader never has to re-read a sentence to understand what you meant.</span> Consistent, distracting errors — not just one typo — are what actually cost points, because they suggest the writer can't reliably control complex sentences, which undermines confidence in the *reasoning* too.

## <span style={{ fontFamily: '"Times New Roman", Times, serif' }}>Which Rule Matters Most?</span>

Of the seven checklist items above, two carry the most weight — but if you can only prioritize one, make it **Subject-Verb Agreement**.

### Why Subject-Verb Agreement Is the Most Powerful Rule

1. **It's the most common error under time pressure.** In a 30-minute essay <span style={{ color: "#7c3aed", fontWeight: 700 }}>with complex sentences (which you *need* for a strong score)</span>, <span style={{ color: "#059669", fontWeight: 700 }}>the subject and verb often get separated by a clause</span> — and <span style={{ color: "#b45309", fontWeight: 700 }}>that's exactly when agreement slips happen</span>, even for strong writers. It's less a knowledge gap and more a speed error, so it shows up disproportionately in timed writing.

2. **It's highly visible to graders.** <span style={{ color: "#7c3aed", fontWeight: 700 }}>Unlike subtler issues (e.g., slightly imprecise word choice), a subject-verb mismatch is an immediate, obvious red flag</span> — it signals the writer isn't fully in control of their sentences, which <span style={{ color: "#059669", fontWeight: 700 }}>undermines trust in the reasoning even if the argument itself is strong.</span>

3. **It compounds with sentence complexity.** The essay rewards varied, sophisticated sentence structures — but sophistication increases the *risk* of agreement errors. This is the rule most likely to break exactly when you're doing what earns you points, making it worth deliberately double-checking.

### Runner-Up: Pronoun Clarity

<span style={{ color: "#b45309", fontWeight: 700 }}>Vague "it / this / they" references don't just cost a grammar point</span> — they can make a grader misread your entire argument, which risks the *content* score, not just mechanics.

### Practical Takeaway

When revising a practice essay (within the ~5-minute revision window), prioritize scanning for these two first: <span style={{ color: "#7c3aed", fontWeight: 700 }}>subject-verb agreement in longer sentences, and confirming every pronoun has one clear antecedent.</span> That's the highest-leverage 60 seconds of editing you can do.
