# GRE

The GRE is not a test of raw intelligence. It's a test of **pattern recognition under time pressure** — for words, for numbers, and for arguments.

Most test-takers prepare by memorizing word lists and drilling random problems. That works up to a point, then plateaus. The better approach — the one this part teaches — is to understand *why* each question type exists, what skill it's actually measuring, and which reusable strategy cracks it every time.

**This track treats the GRE like a system you can engineer**: identify the pattern, apply the strategy, verify the answer, move on.

---

## What This Part Covers

This part is structured around the three sections of the official GRE:

| Chapter | Topic | What You'll Learn |
|---|---|---|
| **Chapter 1** | Verbal Reasoning | Sentence Equivalence, Text Completion, and Reading Comprehension — how to decode dense sentences and predict answers before reading the choices |
| **Chapter 2** | Quantitative Reasoning | Arithmetic, algebra, geometry, and data analysis — the GRE math toolkit, reframed around speed and pattern recognition rather than rote formulas |
| **Chapter 3** | Analytical Writing (AWA) | How to structure the Issue and Argument essays so they score well under a 30-minute clock |

## The Engineering Mindset Applied to the GRE

Great test-taking shares the same properties as great debugging:

- **It is systematic** — every question type has a repeatable strategy, not a one-off trick.
- **It is measurable** — track accuracy and timing per question type, not just overall score.
- **It compounds** — vocabulary, number sense, and essay structure all build on each other across sections.
- **It's about elimination, not certainty** — like ruling out edge cases, ruling out wrong answers is often faster than finding the right one directly.

> **Key insight:** The GRE rewards recognizing question *types*, not memorizing question *content*. Learn the pattern once, and it applies to hundreds of variations on test day.

## Who This Is For

- **Engineers and technical professionals** applying to graduate programs (MS, PhD, MBA with a GRE requirement)
- **Anyone rusty on algebra and geometry** who wants math rebuilt from first principles
- **Non-native English readers** who want vocabulary and sentence structure explained plainly, not just listed
- **Anyone** who wants a study system instead of a stack of flashcards

No prior GRE prep required. If you can reason about systems, you can reason about this test.

---

## How to Use This Part

Each lesson follows the same structure:
1. **What** — the question type or concept, defined clearly
2. **Why** — what skill it's actually testing
3. **How** — the step-by-step strategy, with a worked example
4. **Common Traps** — the wrong answers the test is designed to tempt you with
5. **Practice** — GRE-style questions to apply the strategy

Read Chapter 1 and Chapter 2 in parallel if you're balancing verbal and quant prep. Chapter 3 (AWA) is short enough to finish in a single sitting once the other two are underway.
