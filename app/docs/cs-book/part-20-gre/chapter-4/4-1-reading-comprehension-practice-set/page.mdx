## <span style={{ fontFamily: '"Times New Roman", Times, serif' }}>Practice Set 1: Easy</span>

Questions 1–4 are based on this passage.

> When Johannes Gutenberg developed a practical movable-type printing press in the 1450s, the immediate effect was modest: only a handful of workshops in the German city of Mainz had access to the technology, and books remained expensive luxury items produced in small print runs. Within thirty years, however, printing presses had spread to more than two hundred European cities, and the price of a printed book fell dramatically compared to a hand-copied manuscript. This rapid diffusion did not happen because printing itself became technically easier; rather, it happened because printers who had trained under Gutenberg's associates traveled to new cities and set up their own workshops, teaching the craft to local apprentices in turn. Each new workshop became a training ground for the next generation of printers, producing a chain reaction of technology transfer that spread far faster than any single workshop could have managed on its own.

<a id="easy-q1"></a>**1. (Main Idea)** Which of the following best expresses the main point of the passage? [↓ Jump to Answer](#easy-a1)

A) Gutenberg's press was the single most important invention of the fifteenth century.
B) Printing spread across Europe primarily because trained printers taught the craft to others as they founded new workshops.
C) Books were prohibitively expensive throughout the fifteenth century regardless of printing technology.
D) Printing technology became technically simpler over the thirty years following Gutenberg's invention.
E) Mainz remained the sole center of European printing throughout the fifteenth century.

<a id="easy-q2"></a>**2. (Detail)** According to the passage, why did the price of printed books fall? [↓ Jump to Answer](#easy-a2)

A) The passage does not directly explain the price drop, only that it occurred alongside the technology's rapid spread.
B) Gutenberg lowered his own prices to compete with new workshops.
C) Apprentices were paid less than trained printers.
D) The German government subsidized new print workshops.
E) Manuscript copying became illegal in most European cities.

<a id="easy-q3"></a>**3. (Vocabulary in Context)** As used in the passage, "diffusion" most nearly means [↓ Jump to Answer](#easy-a3)

A) confusion
B) dilution
C) spread
D) argument
E) collapse

<a id="easy-q4"></a>**4. (Inference)** The passage suggests that the speed of printing's spread across Europe was due mainly to [↓ Jump to Answer](#easy-a4)

A) a single workshop rapidly increasing its own output
B) a person-to-person chain of training that multiplied the number of workshops
C) government mandates requiring every city to adopt the technology
D) improvements in the technical design of the printing press itself
E) declining demand for hand-copied manuscripts

## <span style={{ fontFamily: '"Times New Roman", Times, serif' }}>Practice Set 2: Medium</span>

Questions 1–4 are based on this passage.

> Marine biologists have observed a counterintuitive pattern among coral reefs in certain tropical shallow-water regions: reefs that receive moderately reduced sunlight, due to seasonal cloud cover or slightly greater water depth, sometimes support higher overall species diversity than reefs receiving maximal, uninterrupted sunlight exposure. This is puzzling because coral polyps rely on photosynthetic algae living within their tissues, and photosynthesis generally increases with light availability, suggesting that more light should support more coral growth and, by extension, more of the many species that depend on coral structures for habitat. One proposed explanation involves the phenomenon of competitive exclusion: in reefs with maximal light, a small number of fast-growing coral species can outcompete slower-growing, more structurally complex species, ultimately reducing the variety of habitat niches available to other reef organisms. In reefs with moderately reduced light, growth rates are more even across species, preventing any single coral type from dominating and preserving a wider range of structural niches.

<a id="med-q1"></a>**1. (Explain the Paradox)** Which of the following, if true, would best help explain the pattern described in the passage? [↓ Jump to Answer](#med-a1)

A) Fast-growing coral species produce more offspring per year than slow-growing species.
B) Structurally complex coral species provide more distinct habitat niches per unit area than fast-growing species do.
C) Tropical shallow-water regions generally receive more sunlight than deep-water regions.
D) Photosynthetic algae are present in nearly all coral species regardless of growth rate.
E) Species diversity is difficult to measure accurately in underwater environments.

<a id="med-q2"></a>**2. (Select one or more answer choices — consider each choice separately.)** Based on the passage, which of the following would likely be true of a reef where one fast-growing coral species has come to dominate almost the entire reef area? [↓ Jump to Answer](#med-a2)

☐ A) The reef would likely support fewer total habitat niches than a more evenly distributed reef.
☐ B) The reef would receive less total sunlight than a reef with more balanced coral growth.
☐ C) The dominant coral species would likely be receiving close to maximal, uninterrupted sunlight.

<a id="med-q3"></a>**3. (Structure)** The final sentence of the passage ("In reefs with moderately reduced light...") primarily serves to [↓ Jump to Answer](#med-a3)

A) introduce a competing theory that contradicts the passage's main explanation
B) provide a specific example unrelated to competitive exclusion
C) complete the explanation of competitive exclusion by describing the contrasting, lower-light condition
D) summarize the passage's overall conclusion about coral reef conservation
E) question whether competitive exclusion actually occurs in coral reefs

<a id="med-q4"></a>**4. (Inference)** The passage suggests that photosynthesis rate alone is [↓ Jump to Answer](#med-a4)

A) the only factor that determines coral reef species diversity
B) irrelevant to coral growth
C) an insufficient predictor of overall reef species diversity
D) inversely related to coral growth in all conditions
E) impossible to measure in shallow-water reefs

## <span style={{ fontFamily: '"Times New Roman", Times, serif' }}>Practice Set 3: Hard</span>

Questions 1–4 are based on this passage.

> An octopus's nervous system presents an unusual puzzle for researchers studying animal cognition: unlike vertebrates, in which the majority of neurons are concentrated in a centralized brain, roughly two-thirds of an octopus's several hundred million neurons are distributed throughout its eight arms, each of which can independently sense, move, and even continue simple grasping behaviors when severed from the central brain. This distributed architecture has led some researchers to characterize octopus intelligence as fundamentally decentralized, arguing that each arm functions as a semi-autonomous processing unit capable of local decision-making without moment-to-moment instruction from the central brain. Proponents of this view point to experiments in which a severed arm continues to withdraw from noxious stimuli and even reach for food, behaviors that would seem to require centralized coordination in most other animals.
>
> However, more recent studies complicate this picture. When researchers tracked arm movements during natural foraging behavior in intact, undamaged octopuses, they found that the central brain does appear to issue high-level directives, such as which general direction to search, even though it delegates the fine motor details of grasping and manipulation to the arm's local neural circuits. This suggests that octopus cognition may not be purely decentralized after all, but rather organized as a hierarchy in which centralized planning and local autonomous execution operate together, each handling the type of decision it is best suited for.

<a id="hard-q1"></a>**1. (Select one or more answer choices — consider each choice separately.)** Based on the passage, which of the following can be inferred about the central brain of an intact, undamaged octopus? [↓ Jump to Answer](#hard-a1)

☐ A) It plays no meaningful role in the octopus's foraging behavior.
☐ B) It issues general directional guidance during foraging while leaving fine motor control to the arms.
☐ C) It must approve every individual grasping motion the arms perform.

<a id="hard-q2"></a>**2. (Function)** The second paragraph functions primarily to [↓ Jump to Answer](#hard-a2)

A) refute the first paragraph's evidence entirely and replace it with a contradictory theory
B) introduce a complication that revises the first paragraph's fully decentralized view into a hierarchical one
C) provide additional support for the fully decentralized view described in the first paragraph
D) explain the biological mechanism behind neuron distribution in octopus arms
E) argue that octopus cognition cannot be studied using current experimental methods

<a id="hard-q3"></a>**3. (Strengthen/Weaken)** Which of the following findings, if true, would most weaken the "purely decentralized" characterization described in the first paragraph? [↓ Jump to Answer](#hard-a3)

A) A severed octopus arm can continue simple grasping behavior for several minutes.
B) Intact octopuses consistently abandon a foraging direction when the central brain's visual input detects a predator, regardless of what the arms are doing locally.
C) Two-thirds of an octopus's neurons are located outside the central brain.
D) Octopus arms can sense texture and chemical signals independently.
E) Octopus intelligence has been studied less extensively than that of vertebrates.

<a id="hard-q4"></a>**4. (Main Point)** Which of the following best expresses the main point of the passage as a whole? [↓ Jump to Answer](#hard-a4)

A) Octopus arms are entirely independent of the central brain and require no coordination whatsoever.
B) Early evidence suggesting fully decentralized octopus cognition is complicated by later research pointing to a hierarchy of centralized planning and local execution.
C) Octopus cognition is identical to vertebrate cognition in every meaningful respect.
D) The central brain of an octopus contains more neurons than its arms combined.
E) Researchers have reached full consensus on how octopus cognition works.

## <span style={{ fontFamily: '"Times New Roman", Times, serif' }}>Answer Key & Explanations</span>

### Practice Set 1: Easy

<a id="easy-a1"></a>1. **B** [↑ Back to Question 1](#easy-q1) — *Question type: **Main Idea**. Format: **Select One Answer Choice** (5 options, pick exactly one — the standard GRE Reading Comprehension format).* The passage's core claim is that printing spread "because printers... traveled to new cities and set up their own workshops, teaching the craft to local apprentices in turn." (A), (C), and (E) aren't supported; (D) is directly contradicted ("did not happen because printing itself became technically easier").

<a id="easy-a2"></a>2. **A** [↑ Back to Question 2](#easy-q2) — *Question type: **Detail**. Format: **Select One Answer Choice** — this type asks what the passage literally states, not what it implies.* The passage states the price fell but doesn't give a direct causal mechanism for the price drop itself — only for the spread of the technology. Don't assume a cause the passage doesn't state.

<a id="easy-a3"></a>3. **C** [↑ Back to Question 3](#easy-q3) — *Question type: **Vocabulary in Context**. Format: **Select One Answer Choice** — always re-read the sentence with your chosen word substituted in before locking in an answer.* "Diffusion" here describes printing technology spreading person-to-person and city-to-city — "spread" is the closest match.

<a id="easy-a4"></a>4. **B** [↑ Back to Question 4](#easy-q4) — *Question type: **Inference**. Format: **Select One Answer Choice** — the answer must be a logical step beyond the text, not a restatement of it.* <span style={{ color: "#7c3aed", fontWeight: 700 }}>The passage explicitly describes a training chain</span>: printers taught apprentices, who became printers and taught more apprentices — a multiplying, person-to-person mechanism, not a single workshop or top-down mandate.

### Practice Set 2: Medium

<a id="med-a1"></a>1. **B** [↑ Back to Question 1](#med-q1) — *Question type: **Explain the Paradox** (a "resolve the discrepancy" question — you're asked to explain an apparent contradiction, not summarize or infer). Format: **Select One Answer Choice**.* The paradox is that *less* light sometimes means *more* diversity. (B) resolves it: if structurally complex (slower-growing) species provide more habitat niches per area than fast-growing species, then preserving them — as happens under moderately reduced light — would explain higher diversity even with less photosynthesis-driving light.

<a id="med-a2"></a>2. **A and C** [↑ Back to Question 2](#med-q2) — *Question type: **Inference**. Format: **Select One or More Answer Choices** (3 options here; evaluate each independently — you get credit only if you select every correct choice and no incorrect ones, unlike Select One Answer Choice questions).* <span style={{ color: "#059669", fontWeight: 700 }}>(A) is correct</span>: the passage states dominance by one species "ultimately reduc[es] the variety of habitat niches." <span style={{ color: "#059669", fontWeight: 700 }}>(C) is correct</span>: fast-growing species are described as those that thrive under maximal light. (B) is unsupported — the passage never claims dominant-coral reefs receive *less* total sunlight.

<a id="med-a3"></a>3. **C** [↑ Back to Question 3](#med-q3) — *Question type: **Structure**. Format: **Select One Answer Choice** — this type asks what job a specific sentence is doing in the passage's argument, not what it means on its own.* The sentence directly follows and completes the explanation of competitive exclusion by describing what happens under the *opposite* (lower-light) condition — it's not a new theory, an unrelated example, or a summary of the whole passage.

<a id="med-a4"></a>4. **C** [↑ Back to Question 4](#med-q4) — *Question type: **Inference**. Format: **Select One Answer Choice**.* The whole point of the paradox is that more light (and thus more photosynthesis) does *not* straightforwardly predict more diversity — competitive exclusion dynamics matter too. That makes photosynthesis rate <span style={{ color: "#b45309", fontWeight: 700 }}>an insufficient predictor on its own</span>, which is a much narrower claim than "irrelevant" (B) or "the only factor" (A).

### Practice Set 3: Hard

<a id="hard-a1"></a>1. **B only** [↑ Back to Question 1](#hard-q1) — *Question type: **Inference**. Format: **Select One or More Answer Choices** (3 options; each is judged on its own — no partial credit for getting only some of the correct ones right, and no penalty avoidance for picking an extra wrong one).* The passage states the central brain "does appear to issue high-level directives, such as which general direction to search," while delegating "fine motor details... to the arm's local neural circuits." That directly supports (B) and directly contradicts (A) and (C) — the brain plays *some* role but doesn't control every individual motion.

<a id="hard-a2"></a>2. **B** [↑ Back to Question 2](#hard-q2) — *Question type: **Function** (a Structure-family question, applied to a whole paragraph instead of one sentence). Format: **Select One Answer Choice**.* <span style={{ color: "#7c3aed", fontWeight: 700 }}>Signal word "However" at the start of paragraph 2</span> flags a complication, not a refutation or pure support. The paragraph revises the first paragraph's "fully decentralized" framing into a hierarchical model — it doesn't throw out paragraph 1's evidence, it recontextualizes it.

<a id="hard-a3"></a>3. **B** [↑ Back to Question 3](#hard-q3) — *Question type: **Strengthen/Weaken** (borrowed from Critical Reasoning-style logic, occasionally tested inside GRE Reading Comprehension). Format: **Select One Answer Choice** — look for the choice that most directly attacks the argument's core claim, not one that's merely related to the topic.* If intact octopuses reliably override arm-level activity based on centralized visual input (spotting a predator), that shows the central brain exercises real top-down control over the arms' behavior — directly undercutting a "purely decentralized" characterization. (A), (C), and (D) are all facts *from* the decentralized-view paragraph and would, if anything, support it rather than weaken it.

<a id="hard-a4"></a>4. **B** [↑ Back to Question 4](#hard-q4) — *Question type: **Main Point** (like Main Idea, but scoped to the passage as a whole rather than a single paragraph — common on multi-paragraph Hard-tier passages). Format: **Select One Answer Choice**.* This is the passage's overall arc: an initial decentralized characterization, complicated by later evidence pointing to a hierarchy. (A) and (C) are too extreme/unsupported; (D) is a minor detail, not the main point; (E) contradicts the passage's own tone of ongoing complication rather than settled consensus.

## <span style={{ fontFamily: '"Times New Roman", Times, serif' }}>Key Takeaway</span>

<span style={{ color: "#059669", fontWeight: 700 }}>Difficulty on GRE Reading Comprehension climbs through structure, not vocabulary</span> — Easy passages state their point directly, Medium passages ask you to resolve an apparent contradiction, and Hard passages present a claim, then a complication, then ask you to track how the argument actually shifted. <span style={{ color: "#b45309", fontWeight: 700 }}>The skill that scales across all three tiers is the same one from Lesson 1.4: build a structural map on the first read, then use it to answer instead of re-reading from scratch.</span>
