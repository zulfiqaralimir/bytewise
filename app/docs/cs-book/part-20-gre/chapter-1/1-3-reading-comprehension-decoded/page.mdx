# <span style={{ fontFamily: '"Times New Roman", Times, serif' }}><span style={{ color: "#059669" }}>Reading Comprehension Decoded</span>: Five Content Types, Three Answer Formats</span>

<span style={{ color: "#059669", fontWeight: 700 }}>GRE Reading Comprehension questions fall into five content categories, each of which can appear in one of three answer formats.</span> Recognizing which type and format you're facing tells you exactly where to look for the answer and how to approach it.

## <span style={{ fontFamily: '"Times New Roman", Times, serif' }}>Sample Passage</span>

> Urban beekeeping has grown rapidly in major cities over the past decade, driven partly by concerns over declining bee populations and partly by a broader interest in local food production. Proponents argue that rooftop and balcony hives help pollinate city gardens and parks, improving yields for urban farmers. Critics, however, note that concentrated hive placement in dense neighborhoods can strain local nectar and pollen supplies, potentially disadvantaging wild pollinator species that lack the mobility of managed honeybee colonies. Some ecologists now recommend that cities cap the number of hives permitted per district, arguing that unregulated growth may ultimately undermine the very biodiversity urban beekeeping was meant to support.

---

## <span style={{ fontFamily: '"Times New Roman", Times, serif' }}>Part 1: The Five Content Types</span>

### 1. Main Idea

**Asks:** <span style={{ color: "#059669", fontWeight: 700 }}>What is the passage fundamentally about, or what is the author's primary purpose?</span>

**Sample Question:** *Which of the following best states the main point of the passage?*

**Answer:** The passage presents both the benefits and unintended ecological risks of urban beekeeping, concluding with a call for regulation.

**Trap to avoid:** Choosing an answer that's true of only *one paragraph* or *one detail* (e.g., "Wild pollinators lack mobility") rather than the passage as a whole.

---

### 2. Inference

**Asks:** <span style={{ color: "#059669", fontWeight: 700 }}>What must be true based on the passage, even though it isn't stated directly?</span>

**Sample Question:** *It can be inferred from the passage that ecologists recommending hive caps believe:*

**Answer:** Unregulated urban beekeeping could work against overall pollinator biodiversity, not just support it.

**Key rule:** The inference must be a small, safe step from the text — not a guess, and not "real-world" knowledge you happen to have about bees. If it can't be traced back to specific wording in the passage, it's wrong.

---

### 3. Structure

**Asks:** <span style={{ color: "#059669", fontWeight: 700 }}>How is the passage (or a specific paragraph) organized, or how does one part function in relation to the whole?</span>

**Sample Question:** *The passage's discussion of critics' concerns primarily serves to:*

**Answer:** Introduce a counterpoint that complicates the initially positive framing of urban beekeeping.

**What to look for:** The passage's shape — here, it moves from *claim* (beekeeping is beneficial) → *complication* (critics' concern) → *proposed resolution* (regulation).

---

### 4. Specific Detail

**Asks:** <span style={{ color: "#059669", fontWeight: 700 }}>About a fact stated directly somewhere in the passage — no inference required.</span>

**Sample Question:** *According to the passage, what is one reason given for the rise in urban beekeeping?*

**Answer:** Concerns over declining bee populations.

**Trap to avoid:** Answer choices that are *almost* right — reworded slightly, or accurate but about the wrong detail (e.g., attributing the concern to "declining crop yields" instead of "declining bee populations").

---

### 5. Vocabulary-in-Context

**Asks:** <span style={{ color: "#059669", fontWeight: 700 }}>What a specific word means *as used in the passage* — not its general dictionary definition.</span>

**Sample Question:** *In the context of the passage, the word "concentrated" (as in "concentrated hive placement") most nearly means:*

**Answer:** Densely clustered in a small area.

**Trap to avoid:** Picking the word's most common everyday meaning (e.g., "concentrated" as in "focused mentally") instead of the meaning that fits this specific sentence.

---

## <span style={{ fontFamily: '"Times New Roman", Times, serif' }}>Part 2: The Three Answer Formats</span>

Any of the five content types above can appear in one of these formats.

### Format 1: Standard Single-Answer

Pick one correct choice out of five. This is the default format and applies to most Main Idea, Inference, Structure, and Detail questions — as shown in the examples above.

### Format 2: Multiple-Answer ("Select All That Apply")

<span style={{ color: "#059669", fontWeight: 700 }}>Three answer choices are given, and you must select **every** correct one</span> — it could be one, two, or all three. There is no partial credit: missing a correct choice or including a wrong one means the whole question is marked incorrect.

**Sample Question:** *Which of the following are presented in the passage as potential drawbacks of urban beekeeping? Select all that apply.*

A) Strain on local nectar and pollen supplies
B) Disadvantage to wild pollinator species
C) Increased cost of honey production

**Answer:** A and B only. (C is never mentioned in the passage — a classic trap, since it *sounds* plausible but isn't supported by the text.)

### Format 3: Select-in-Passage

Instead of multiple-choice options, you click directly on the sentence within the passage that answers the question.

**Sample Question:** *Select the sentence in the passage that presents the ecologists' proposed solution to the concerns raised by critics.*

**Answer:** *"Some ecologists now recommend that cities cap the number of hives permitted per district, arguing that unregulated growth may ultimately undermine the very biodiversity urban beekeeping was meant to support."*

**Strategy note:** Since there are no answer choices to eliminate, <span style={{ color: "#059669", fontWeight: 700 }}>this format rewards knowing the passage's *structure* well</span> — you need to already know which paragraph does what before you can locate the right sentence quickly.

---

## <span style={{ fontFamily: '"Times New Roman", Times, serif' }}>The Common Thread</span>

For all five content types and all three formats, <span style={{ color: "#059669", fontWeight: 700 }}>the correct answer must be traceable back to the passage itself</span> — never to outside knowledge, personal experience, or "what's generally true," no matter how reasonable it sounds.

**Full picture:** 5 content categories (Main Idea, Inference, Structure, Specific Detail, Vocabulary-in-Context) × 3 answer formats (Standard, Select all that apply, Select-in-passage). Any content category can appear in any format.
