## What Is CGE Modelling?

CGE (Computable General Equilibrium) models are mathematical representations of an entire economy that show how sectors, households, firms, and government interact with one another. Think of it as a detailed simulation of an economy.

**Key characteristics:**

- **"Computable"** — it uses actual data and can be solved numerically on computers.
- **"General"** — it covers the whole economy, not just one market.
- **"Equilibrium"** — it assumes markets clear (supply equals demand).

**What it's used for:**

- Analyzing tax policy changes (e.g., "What happens if we increase VAT?")
- Trade policy (impacts of tariffs or free trade agreements)
- Environmental policies (carbon taxes, emissions trading)
- Development planning
- Income distribution effects

**A simple analogy:** Imagine the economy as a complex machine with many gears (sectors) connected to each other. If you turn one gear (change one policy), a CGE model helps predict how all the other gears will move in response.
