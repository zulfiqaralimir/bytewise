# Introduction to CGE Modelling — Part 2: Theoretical Foundations

> *Economics & Finance Models · CGE Modelling Series · Part 2 of 13*

Part 1 defined what a CGE model is and what it's used for. This part covers the microeconomic theory the model is built on.

---

## Theoretical Foundations

CGE models are built on economic theory, primarily from microeconomics.

### A. Walrasian General Equilibrium

Named after economist Léon Walras: all markets in an economy are interconnected and reach equilibrium simultaneously. When one market changes, it ripples through the entire economy.

### B. Key Economic Agents

1. **Households** — consume goods, supply labour, save money
2. **Firms/Producers** — use inputs (labour, capital) to produce goods and services
3. **Government** — collects taxes, provides services, may subsidize
4. **Rest of the World (ROW)** — handles imports and exports

### C. Core Assumptions

1. **Rational behavior** — agents maximize utility (households) or profits (firms)
2. **Market clearing** — supply = demand in all markets (prices adjust to make this happen)
3. **Zero excess profits** — in the long run, firms earn normal profits
4. **Budget constraints** — you can't spend more than you earn (households and government)

### D. The Circular Flow

Households earn income from selling labour and capital to firms. They spend this income on goods and services. Firms use revenues to pay for inputs. This creates a continuous circular flow of money and goods.

**Simple example:** If the government raises income tax, households have less to spend → demand for goods falls → firms produce less → they hire fewer workers → household income falls further (a multiplier effect).
