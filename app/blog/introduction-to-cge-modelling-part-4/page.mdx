# Introduction to CGE Modelling — Part 4: How CGE Models Are Calibrated

> *Economics & Finance Models · CGE Modelling Series · Part 4 of 13*

Part 3 laid out the model's structure — SAM, equations, closures. This part covers calibration: how that structure gets tuned to match a real economy.

---

## How CGE Models Are Calibrated

Calibration is the process of making a model match real-world data. Unlike econometric models (estimated statistically), CGE models are usually **calibrated** to a base year — tuned so that running the model with no shocks exactly reproduces the base-year SAM.

### The Calibration Process

**Step 1 — Gather the SAM.** Base-year values for production, consumption, trade, from national accounts, input-output tables, household surveys.

**Step 2 — Choose Functional Forms.** Production functions (Cobb-Douglas, CES, Leontief); demand functions (LES, CES, etc.).

**Step 3 — Assign Elasticities.** These come from outside literature (econometric studies):

- **Substitution elasticity (σ):** how easily one input substitutes for another
- **Income elasticity:** how demand changes with income
- **Armington elasticity:** how easily domestic and imported goods substitute

Example: if σ = 0.5 for labour–capital substitution, it's harder to replace workers with machines; if σ = 2, it's easier.

**Step 4 — Calculate Share Parameters.** Using the SAM and chosen elasticities, calculate the "share parameters" so the model exactly replicates the base year.

### Worked Example (Cobb-Douglas)

$$Q = A \cdot L^{\alpha} \cdot K^{\beta}$$

From the SAM: Q₀ = 100, L₀ = 50, K₀ = 40. Set α = labour's cost share = 0.6, β = 0.4. Solve for A so that 100 = A · 50^0.6 · 40^0.4.

### Benchmark Equilibrium

After calibration, running the model with no shocks should reproduce the base year exactly — this proves calibration worked.

### Limitations of Calibration

- The model is "locked" to one year of data
- It relies heavily on elasticities from the literature
- It may not capture structural change over time
