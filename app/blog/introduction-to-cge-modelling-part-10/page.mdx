# Introduction to CGE Modelling — Part 10: Model EXTER — An Open Economy with Government

> *Economics & Finance Models · CGE Modelling Series · Part 10 of 13*

Part 9 covered Model 0, the minimal five-account teaching model. This part covers the fully-developed end point of the MIMAP sequence: Model EXTER.

---

## Model EXTER: An Open Economy with Government

Model EXTER is the fully-developed end point of the MIMAP sequence (Model 0 → AUTA → AUTETA → EXTER): a 19-account SAM that opens the economy to trade and adds a government sector, splitting households into labour-endowed and capital-endowed types, adding firms, and separating domestic-market from export-market transactions for each of four sectors (Agriculture, Industry, Services, Non-tradables).

### Reading the SAM, Block by Block

The same rule from Part 8 applies here: reading **across a row** shows what that account *receives*; reading **down a column** shows what it *pays out*. Below, every figure is read across a row — i.e., as a receipt.

**Factors (Rows 1–2): what Labour and Capital collect**

Row 1 (Labour) receives 5,760 + 7,560 + 8,580 + 6,960 = 28,860 — wages paid by Agriculture, Industry, Services, and Non-tradables respectively. Row 2 (Capital) receives 1,440 + 11,340 + 5,720 = 18,500 from the same three tradable activities; Non-tradables pays no capital return at all, consistent with the "labour-only" note in the symbols table above.

**Households and Firms (Rows 3–5): who owns the factors**

Row 3 (Labour-endowed households) receives 28,860 from the Labour account plus 140 in remittances from the Rest of World, for 29,000 total. Row 4 (Capital-endowed households) receives 11,100 from the Capital account plus 1,900 in dividends from Firms, for 13,000. Row 5 (Firms) receives 6,475 — the capital income Firms collect before passing most of it on to Capital-endowed households as dividends.

**Government (Row 6): tax revenue**

Row 6 (Government) receives 580 (direct tax on Labour-HH) + 650 (direct tax on Capital-HH) + 1,295 (tax on Firms) + 190.24 (indirect tax on Agriculture) + 7,069.22 (indirect tax on Industry) + 675 (indirect tax on Services) + 90 (indirect tax on Non-tradables) = 10,549.46. Notice how lopsided this is: Industry alone supplies two-thirds of all indirect tax revenue.

**Rest of World (Row 7): what foreigners pay Pakistan**

Row 7 (Rest of World) receives 925 (capital repatriated abroad) + 370 (firm payments abroad) + 379 (Agriculture imports) + 15,300 (Industry imports) + 2,700 (Services imports) = 19,674 — money leaving the country to pay for capital returns and imports.

**Activities (Rows 8–11): sales revenue, domestic plus export**

Each activity's row total is its gross output, split between what it sells domestically and what it exports:

| Activity | Domestic sales | Export sales | Total output |
|---|---|---|---|
| Agriculture (8) | 7,200 | 1,800 | 9,000 |
| Industry (9) | 46,240 | 8,160 | 54,400 |
| Services (10) | 19,800 | 2,200 | 22,000 |
| Non-tradables (11) | 8,700 | — | 8,700 |

Non-tradables has no export figure at all — there's no column for it, because (as Part 12 covers) it can't be traded internationally by definition.

**Domestic markets (Rows 12–14): who buys the tradable goods**

Each domestic-good row is bought by two kinds of buyer: households consuming it directly, and activities using it as an intermediate input (plus, for Industry, a large slice going to investment). For Industry's domestic good (Row 13, total 68,609.22 approx.): Agriculture buys 1,504 as an input, Industry buys 29,616.81 of its own output back as an input (steel used to make more steel-based goods), Services buys 5,459.45, Non-tradables buys 1,336.05, and 15,619.91 goes to investment (Accumulation) — machinery and equipment. The remainder, roughly 15,000, is direct household consumption. Non-tradables' own domestic good (Row 15, 8,700) is different: it's absorbed entirely within the local economy, with no investment or export component at all.

**Export markets (Rows 16–18): what leaves the country**

These rows are the simplest — each is a single number flowing to the Rest of World: Agriculture exports 1,890, Industry 8,160, Services 2,200. (Compare this to the *domestic-market* sales rows above, which are $8{,}700 - 1{,}890$ etc. smaller than each activity's gross output — the export columns 16–18 and domestic columns 12–14 together account for a sector's full output.)

**Accumulation (Row 19): total savings**

Row 19 receives 2,842 (Labour-HH savings) + 2,470 (Capital-HH savings) + 2,910 (Firm savings) + 1,709.46 (Government savings) + 7,424 (foreign savings, i.e. the current account) = 17,355.46 — every institution's unspent income, pooled and ready to fund investment.

**The balance check**

Every one of these row totals has a matching column total elsewhere in the SAM — Labour's 28,860 in receipts is exactly matched by 28,860 in payments (all of it passed on to Labour-HH); Government's 10,549.46 in tax revenue is matched by 10,549.46 in transfers, consumption, and savings. That row-equals-column balance, checked account by account, is what confirms the SAM is internally consistent before it's used to calibrate the model.

### Reading What Changed from Model 0 to EXTER

Compared with Model 0's five accounts, EXTER adds three structural features:

1. **Split households and firms** — labour-endowed households (row/column 3) and capital-endowed households (4) receive factor income separately, and firms (5) sit between capital and its owners, paying dividends ($DIV$) and retaining some earnings.
2. **A government sector** (6) — collecting direct taxes ($DTH_{HW}, DTH_{HC}, DTF$) and indirect taxes ($TI_A, TI_I, TI_S$), paying transfers ($TG$) and consuming ($G$).
3. **A genuinely open trade structure** — each tradable sector (Agriculture, Industry, Services) is split into a domestic-market row/column (12–14) and an export-market row/column (16–18), fed by the Armington and CET equations described in Part 12, with imports ($IM$) and capital repatriation ($RK_{row}, DIV_{row}$) flowing to and from the Rest of World (7).

The accumulation account (19) closes the model by collecting all savings — household, firm, government, and foreign (the current account balance, $CAB$) — and channelling them into investment demand ($IV_A, IV_I$) in the domestic market.
