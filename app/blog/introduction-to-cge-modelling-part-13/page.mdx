# Introduction to CGE Modelling — Part 13: Conclusion

> *Economics & Finance Models · CGE Modelling Series · Part 13 of 13*

This closes out the series. Here's how everything from Parts 1–12 fits together.

---

## Conclusion

CGE modelling connects economic theory (general equilibrium, utility and profit maximization, market clearing) to real, country-specific data (the Social Accounting Matrix) through a calibrated system of equations — production, demand, trade, income distribution, and market-clearing conditions — that can be solved before and after a policy shock to trace out its economy-wide effects.

The path covered here — from the basic definition of CGE, through SAM structure and reading conventions, calibration, Model 0's five-account SAM and twelve core equations, the fully open nineteen-account Model EXTER, sector-specific CES elasticities, and Armington/CET trade behaviour — traces the standard MIMAP training sequence (Model 0 → AUTA → AUTETA → EXTER) and, applied throughout to Pakistan's economy, gives a concrete foundation for building toward a full country CGE model in GAMS.
