# Introduction to CGE Modelling — Part 3: Structure and Components of a CGE Model

> *Economics & Finance Models · CGE Modelling Series · Part 3 of 13*

Part 2 covered the theory. This part opens up the model itself — its data, its equations, and the closure rules that shape how it behaves.

---

## Structure and Components of a CGE Model

### A. The Database — the Social Accounting Matrix (SAM)

This is the foundation: a snapshot of the economy in a base year showing all transactions between agents, production by sector, consumption patterns, trade flows, and tax revenues. It is a detailed spreadsheet of the entire economy where every row total equals its corresponding column total.

### B. Equations in the Model

1. **Production Functions** — combine inputs (labour, capital, materials) into output, e.g. via nested CES functions.
2. **Demand Functions** — households maximize utility subject to budget; firms minimize cost. Often Linear Expenditure System (LES) or CES functions.
3. **Trade Equations** — the Armington assumption treats domestic and foreign goods as imperfect substitutes; export supply and import demand functions follow.
4. **Market Clearing Conditions** — for each good: Production + Imports = Consumption + Investment + Exports; for factors: Labour supply = Labour demand across all sectors.
5. **Income–Expenditure Identities** — household income = wages + capital income + transfers − taxes; government budget: tax revenue = spending + transfers (or deficit/surplus).
6. **Pricing Equations** — relate producer prices, consumer prices, taxes, and world prices.

### C. Closures

"Closure rules" determine which variables are fixed (exogenous) and which adjust (endogenous):

- **Labour market:** Is the wage fixed, or employment? Or do both adjust?
- **Government:** Does it balance its budget, or run a deficit?
- **Investment–Savings:** How is investment determined?

Different closures produce different model behaviour.
