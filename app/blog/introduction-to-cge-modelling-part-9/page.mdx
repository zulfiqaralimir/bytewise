# Introduction to CGE Modelling — Part 9: Model 0 — The Twelve Core Equations

> *Economics & Finance Models · CGE Modelling Series · Part 9 of 13*

Parts 1–8 covered theory, structure, calibration, simulation, a worked example, tools, and how to read a SAM. This part introduces the first of two teaching models from the MIMAP training sequence: Model 0.

---

## Model 0: The Twelve Core Equations

MIMAP's Model 0 is a minimal theoretical CGE model built around a 5-account SAM (Labour, Capital, Households, Goods, Services), used to teach the mechanics before moving to larger models (AUTA, AUTETA, EXTER).

### The Model 0 SAM Structure

| Receipts → / Expenses ↓ | Factors: 1. | Factors: 2. | Institutions: 3. | Prod. Activities: 4. | Prod. Activities: 5. | Total (1 to 5) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Labour** | | | | $WL_G$ | $WL_S$ | $WL$ |
| **2. Capital** | | | | $RK_G$ | $RK_S$ | $RK$ |
| **3. Households** | $WL$ | $RK$ | | | | $YH$ |
| **4. Goods** | | | $E_G$ | | | $VX_G$ |
| **5. Services** | | | $E_S$ | | | $VX_S$ |
| **Total (1 to 5)** | $WL$ | $RK$ | $YH$ | $VX_G$ | $VX_S$ | |

**Legend:**

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| $i, j \in \{G, S\}$ | Activities and products (G: Goods, S: Services) |
| $E_i$ | Households' expenditure on good $i$ |
| $RK_j$ | Rate of return to capital in activity $j$ |
| $RK$ | Total capital revenue |
| $VX_j$ | Output of activity $j$ |
| $WL_j$ | Wage bill in activity $j$ |
| $WL$ | Total wage bill paid to workers |
| $YH$ | Households' income |

### <span style={{ color: "#2563eb", fontWeight: 700 }}>Labour, Capital, and Households: Three Different Accounts</span>

It's easy to look at this SAM and assume Labour and Capital *are* the households — after all, households are the ones working and owning things. But the SAM tracks them as three separate accounts because each answers a different question.

**Labour (Factor Account 1) — the supply of work**

```text
Labour is a PRODUCTIVE FACTOR (input to production)
├─ It represents: total hours of work available
├─ Who owns it? Households own their own labour
├─ What happens?
│   ├─ Households SUPPLY their labour to firms
│   └─ Firms PAY WAGES for that labour
└─ In the SAM: tracks total wage payments (WL = 28,860)
```

**Capital (Factor Account 2) — productive assets**

```text
Capital is a PRODUCTIVE FACTOR (input to production)
├─ It represents: machines, equipment, buildings, land
├─ Who owns it? Households own capital through savings/investments
├─ What happens?
│   ├─ Households SUPPLY their capital to firms
│   └─ Firms PAY RETURNS for that capital (profits, rent, interest)
└─ In the SAM: tracks total capital payments (RK = 18,500)
```

**Households (Institution Account 3) — the people who own both**

```text
Households are ECONOMIC AGENTS (decision-makers)
├─ They OWN labour (their work capacity)
├─ They OWN capital (savings, property, investments)
├─ They RECEIVE income from selling labour + capital
├─ They DECIDE how much to spend vs. save
├─ They CONSUME goods and services
└─ In the SAM: tracks income received + spending decisions
```

The relationship between the three:

```text
                    HOUSEHOLDS
                    (the people)
                    ↙        ↘
              OWN LABOUR      OWN CAPITAL
              (their work)    (their assets)
                  ↓               ↓
            SUPPLY TO FIRMS  SUPPLY TO FIRMS
                  ↓               ↓
            RECEIVE WAGES   RECEIVE RETURNS
             (WL = 28,860)   (RK = 18,500)
                  ↓               ↓
            TOTAL INCOME (YH = 47,360)
                  ↓
            DECIDE TO SPEND
            on Goods + Services
```

**A concrete example:** Consider a household member, Ahmed, who both works and owns a small asset.

- **As a worker (labour supply):** Ahmed works 8 hours/day at a factory. The factory pays him 500 PKR/day. That 500 PKR is part of $WL_G$ — labour payment to the Goods sector.
- **As a capital owner (capital supply):** Ahmed also owns a small shop building, which he rents out for 2,000 PKR/month (≈ 67 PKR/day). That 67 PKR/day is part of $RK_G$ — capital return from the Goods sector.
- **As a household member (consumer):** Ahmed's total income is 500 + 67 = 567 PKR/day. He spends 340 PKR on food (Goods), 180 PKR on services, and saves 47 PKR. That 520 PKR of spending is part of $E_G$ and $E_S$.

In the SAM, Ahmed's wage is counted in the Labour account, his rental income in the Capital account, and his spending in the Households account — three different rows even though it's the same person in all three.

**Why the SAM separates them:**

| | Labour & Capital | Households |
|---|---|---|
| What | Resources used in production | The people/consumers |
| Role | Supply side of the economy | Demand side of the economy |
| Question answered | "Where do production inputs come from?" | "Who decides what to consume?" |
| Answer | From households who own them | Households, based on their income |

Or, in national-accounting terms: the Labour and Capital accounts answer "what did firms pay for inputs?"; the Households account answers "what did people earn and spend?"

**The distinction, side by side:**

```text
WRONG WAY (a common first read):
Households = Labour + Capital + Consumption
(all mixed together)

CORRECT WAY (SAM structure):
FACTORS (inputs to production):
  ├─ Labour  = flow of work    (supply side)
  └─ Capital = stock of assets (supply side)

INSTITUTIONS (decision-makers & consumers):
  └─ Households = people who own factors & consume (demand side)
```

**Tracing Ahmed's money through the SAM:**

```text
Ahmed works                         → Labour account
Ahmed earns 567 PKR/day             → Households account (income)
Ahmed spends 520 PKR                → Households account (spending)
Money flows to Goods and Services   → Production accounts
Which hire labour and capital again → Labour / Capital accounts
Ahmed gets paid again                → circular flow continues
```

**Summary:**

| Account | Represents | Role | Flow direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Labour | Work/labour supply | Factor of production | Households → Activities |
| Capital | Assets/machinery | Factor of production | Households → Activities |
| Households | People/consumers | Economic agent | Factors → Households → Activities |

Same people, three roles: as workers they show up in the Labour account, as asset owners in the Capital account, and as consumers in the Households account.

### Model 0 Numerical Example

| Receipts → / Expenses ↓ | Factors: 1. | Factors: 2. | Institutions: 3. | Prod. Activities: 4. | Prod. Activities: 5. | Total (1 to 5) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Labour** | | | | 13 320 | 15 540 | 28 860 |
| **2. Capital** | | | | 12 780 | 5 720 | 18 500 |
| **3. Households** | 28 860 | 18 500 | | | | 47 360 |
| **4. Goods** | | | 26 100 | | | 26 100 |
| **5. Services** | | | 21 260 | | | 21 260 |
| **Total (1 to 5)** | 28 860 | 18 500 | 47 360 | 26 100 | 21 260 | |

This single numerical SAM is what the twelve equations below are calibrated against: $WL = 28\,860$, $RK = 18\,500$, $YH = 47\,360$, output of Goods $VX_G = 26\,100$, output of Services $VX_S = 21\,260$ — and every row total equals its column total, confirming the SAM balances before it's used for calibration.

| Eq. | Formula | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | $XS_i = A_i (LD_i)^{\alpha_i} (KD_i)^{(1-\alpha_i)}$ | Production (Cobb-Douglas) |
| 2 | $LD_i = \dfrac{\alpha_i P_i XS_i}{w}$ | Labour Demand |
| 3 | $KD_i = \dfrac{(1-\alpha_i) P_i XS_i}{r_i}$ | Capital Demand |
| 4 | $YH = w \times LD_{\text{total}} + \sum_i r_i KD_i$ | Household Income |
| 5 | $C_i = \beta_i \times YH$ | Consumption |
| 6 | $XS_i = C_i + IC_i$ | Goods Market Clearing |
| 7 | $L^S = \sum_i LD_i$ | Labour Market Clearing |
| 8 | $K^S = \sum_i KD_i$ | Capital Market Clearing |
| 9 | $VA_i = w \times LD_i + r_i KD_i$ | Value Added |
| 10 | $YH = E + S$ | Income–Expenditure Balance |
| 11 | $YH = W + R$ | Factor Income Distribution |
| 12 | $w = 1$ | Price Numeraire |

**What each block of equations does:**

- Equations 1–3: how production works (Cobb-Douglas)
- Equation 4: how households earn income
- Equation 5: how households spend income
- Equations 6–8: markets clear (supply = demand)
- Equations 9–11: accounting identities balance
- Equation 12: sets the price baseline

### <span style={{ color: "#2563eb", fontWeight: 700 }}>Working Through the Equations</span>

The table above states each relationship; this section shows where three of them come from, and works two through with the benchmark numbers.

**Equations 1–3 — production and factor demand.** Firms choose $LD_i$ and $KD_i$ to minimize the cost $w \cdot LD_i + r_i \cdot KD_i$ of producing a target output $XS_i$ under the Cobb-Douglas technology in Equation 1. The first-order condition of that cost-minimization problem gives Equations 2 and 3 directly: each factor's demand equals its cost share of total revenue, divided by its own price.

Reading the shares straight off the benchmark SAM:

$$\alpha_G = \frac{WL_G}{VX_G} = \frac{13{,}320}{26{,}100} \approx 0.51, \qquad 1-\alpha_G = \frac{RK_G}{VX_G} = \frac{12{,}780}{26{,}100} \approx 0.49$$

$$\alpha_S = \frac{WL_S}{VX_S} = \frac{15{,}540}{21{,}260} \approx 0.73, \qquad 1-\alpha_S = \frac{RK_S}{VX_S} = \frac{5{,}720}{21{,}260} \approx 0.27$$

With the wage numeraire $w=1$, Goods' labour demand collapses to $LD_G = \alpha_G \cdot P_G \cdot XS_G = WL_G = 13{,}320$ at the benchmark — the equation reproduces the SAM exactly, which is what calibration (Part 4) requires by construction.

**Equation 4 — household income.** Households collect every wage and capital payment made anywhere in the economy:

$$YH = w \cdot LD_{\text{total}} + \sum_i r_i KD_i = 28{,}860 + 18{,}500 = 47{,}360$$

**Equation 5 — where $C_i = \beta_i \cdot YH$ comes from.** Households are assumed to maximize a Cobb-Douglas utility function over the two goods, $U = C_G^{\beta_G} \cdot C_S^{\beta_S}$, subject to the budget constraint $P_G C_G + P_S C_S = YH$. Setting up the Lagrangian, taking the first-order condition on each $C_i$, and eliminating the multiplier collapses to a strikingly simple result: households spend a *fixed share* $\beta_i$ of income on each good, regardless of price. This is the same trick behind Cobb-Douglas production — constant output elasticities become constant expenditure shares. The shares $\beta_i$ are read straight off the SAM's consumption column ($E_G, E_S$) divided by $YH$, and by construction $\sum_i \beta_i = 1$.

**Equations 6–8 — market clearing.** $XS_i = C_i + IC_i$: output covers final consumption plus what other sectors use as an input (intermediate consumption). $L^S = \sum_i LD_i$ and $K^S = \sum_i KD_i$ are cleared by *prices*, not quantities — if labour demand exceeded supply, the wage $w$ would rise until firms cut back to $L^S$; if capital demand fell short of supply, the return $r_i$ would fall until firms absorbed the rest. At the benchmark, both already hold exactly: $LD_G+LD_S=28{,}860=L^S$ and $KD_G+KD_S=18{,}500=K^S$.

**Equations 9–11 — the identities that must balance.** $VA_i = w\,LD_i + r_i\,KD_i$ restates value added as factor payments: $VA_G = 13{,}320+12{,}780=26{,}100=VX_G$, confirming Goods has no separate profit margin beyond what it pays labour and capital. $YH=E+S$ and $YH=W+R$ are two ways of splitting the same number — by use (spent vs. saved) and by source (wages vs. capital returns). Both reconcile to $YH=47{,}360$ automatically once Equations 1–9 are solved; they're accounting checks on the solution, not additional constraints to solve for.

**Equation 12 — the numeraire.** Covered in full below.

### Why a Numeraire Is Needed

Without a numeraire, the model is underdetermined: with prices $P_8, P_9, P_{10}, P_{11}, w, r_8, r_9, r_{10}, r_{11}$ as nine unknowns, equations 1–11 only give eight independent equations. Fixing one price — the numeraire — closes the system. Common choices:

- **Wage as numeraire:** $w = 1$; all other prices are read relative to the wage.
- **Price of goods as numeraire:** $P_8 = 1$.
- **Price index as numeraire:** $P_{\text{index}} = \sum_i \beta_i P_i = 1$ (a weighted average of prices equals 1).

For example, with $w=1$, a solved model might give $P_8 = 2$, $P_9 = 1.5$, $P_{10} = 0.8$ — meaning agriculture costs twice the wage, industry 1.5×, and services 0.8× the wage.

### Solving the Model

**Inputs:** labour supply $L^S$, capital supply $K^S$, and parameters $\alpha_i, \beta_i, A_i$.

**Solve the 12 equations simultaneously** to obtain prices ($P_8, P_9, P_{10}, P_{11}, w, r_8, r_9, r_{10}$), quantities ($XS_i, LD_i, KD_i, C_i$), and income ($YH, W, R$).
