Welcome and Course Overview

The scenario you'll carry through every course, why thinking comes before tools, and the week-by-week plan for Course 1.

The scenario you'll picture throughout

  • Throughout these courses, picture yourself in a particular scenario.
  • You've just been hired as a data engineer at an exciting new company in the e-commerce space. (Congratulations on the new job!)

Why the company hired you

  • The company first hired a data scientist because they wanted to do analytics to learn about their customers':
    • Interests
    • Behaviors
    • Purchasing habits
  • They also wanted to develop machine learning tools using large language models to automate various aspects of customer support.
  • But when the data scientist started, they found that none of the data infrastructure needed for the analytics or ML tasks even existed yet.
  • They started studying data architectures and systems to try to build the infrastructure themselves.
  • They also told leadership that building data infrastructure wasn't their forte, and that to optimize for success, the company should also hire a data engineer.
  • That's where you come in.

Joe's own story

  • It might sound like a silly story, but Joe has seen this exact scenario play out in real life many times.
  • In fact, Joe was one of those data scientists hired by a company just like this one.
  • He was thrown into a situation where he had to quickly learn to build various parts of data infrastructure, just to get the company's data and systems into a state where he could start the data science projects he was hired for.
  • That was many years ago, when the role of data engineer didn't even exist yet.
    • So the company could be forgiven for not knowing what they really needed.
  • Diving into designing and building data systems, he was both overwhelmed and excited by what he learned.

The core skills are the same

  • He discovered that the core skills needed to build robust data infrastructure are largely the same no matter the downstream task, whether that's:
    • Data science
    • Machine learning
    • Delivering embedded analytics to users within applications
  • Over time, that core set of skills has come to be known as data engineering.

Your job as the data engineer

  • Your company wants you to build data systems that deliver on their goals.
  • To do this, you'll need to:
    • Translate the needs of stakeholders into system requirements
    • Then choose the right set of tools and technologies to build that system

The trap: tools before understanding

  • It sounds straightforward, but Joe has often seen data engineers dive straight into implementation.
  • They choose tools and technologies before deeply understanding how the system will deliver value for the organization.
  • That approach can lead to disaster:
    • Wasting time
    • Wasting company resources
    • Even costing you your job

How these courses approach it

  • Rather than diving straight into implementation, these courses (especially Course 1) spend a fair amount of time on the big picture:
    • A set of principles that apply to all your data engineering projects
    • Forming a mental framework for building successful data systems
  • Don't worry, you'll also get into implementation.
  • In the lab exercises, you'll work with cutting-edge tools and technologies to build data systems on the AWS cloud.

Plan for Course 1

Week 1: High-level look at the field of data engineering

  • Data engineering lifecycle
  • History of data engineering
  • The data engineer among other stakeholders: where data engineering sits alongside other roles in your company
  • Business value: what it means to add value to the business as a data engineer
  • Translation of stakeholder needs into requirements: gathering needs and turning them into system requirements, a theme revisited throughout these courses

An important heads-up about Week 1

  • You won't write any code this week.
  • You won't use any cloud tools this week.
  • This week is all about how to think like a data engineer.
  • It may feel different from what you expected when signing up, but thinking like a data engineer is step one to succeeding in this field.
  • With the right high-level knowledge and mindset, all the rest of successful data engineering becomes possible.
  • Without it, no amount of technical know-how or coding skill can save you.
  • The specialization will go deep on the technical side too, so that's coming.

Week 2: Data engineering lifecycle and undercurrents

  • A deep dive into each stage of the data engineering lifecycle.
  • Also contains a lot of theory and high-level orientation, so brace yourself.
  • You'll build on thinking like a data engineer.
  • Then you'll get hands-on practice with cloud data pipelines on AWS in the lab at the end of the week.

Week 3: Principles of good data architecture

  • Focus on the principles of good data architecture.

Week 4: Design and build out a data architecture

  • Pull it all together to design and build a data architecture that delivers value based on stakeholder needs.
