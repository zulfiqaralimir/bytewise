Welcome to Data Engineering

An intro conversation between the host and instructor Joe Reis on why data engineering matters, what you'll learn, and how the program is laid out.

Why data engineering matters now

  • In the last decade, pretty much every industry has gone digital.
  • Digital communications are everywhere, and digital data is replacing paper as the main way information is stored in:
    • Healthcare
    • Finance
    • Manufacturing
    • Education
    • Tech
    • Pretty much every other industry
  • This tidal wave of data brings:
    • Risks
    • New challenges in how to store and process all this data to create value
  • Whether an organization wants to serve customers better or outmaneuver the competition, good data pipelines are increasingly critical.
  • That's why there's skyrocketing demand for data engineers.

Meet the instructor: Joe Reis

  • Data engineer and data architect with decades of experience helping companies with their data needs.
  • Also a professor and podcaster.
  • Co-author of the best-selling book Fundamentals of Data Engineering.

What is a "recovering data scientist"?

  • It's a nickname Joe was given around the mid-2010s, when data science was becoming very hot and popular.
  • His background was in machine learning, analytics, and similar roles.
  • What he noticed:
    • Companies were hiring data scientists without giving them proper support and foundational infrastructure.
    • Data scientists often started jobs with no data to actually do science on.
  • After seeing this again and again, he realized data engineering was key to making data science succeed.
  • Data engineering means building the foundation:
    • Infrastructure
    • Data quality
    • The means for data scientists to do their job
  • So he started in data science, then moved into data engineering to solve these problems for data scientists.

Speed and efficiency: why the foundation matters

  • With a large dataset, if a data scientist can ask a question and get an answer in five minutes or an hour instead of a week or a month, that creates massive efficiency differences.
  • Often, the difference comes down to whether data engineering is there to support rapid development.
  • Companies now see data as an invaluable asset, so they're investing in that foundational layer again.
  • The goal: cut time to value from days, weeks, months down to hours, minutes, sometimes seconds.

Small data vs. big data

  • A small CSV file on your laptop is manageable, though it still has some complexities.
  • Once you go beyond small data, the complexity in the pipeline makes a huge difference in performance.

Data engineering in the age of AI

  • With the rise of AI, it's more important than ever to take a data-centric first approach.
  • There's finally recognition that data, infrastructure, and methodologies to support data truly matter.
  • Since AI is now front and center in every company and every workload, Joe feels this is the time for data engineering.

What is data-centric AI?

  • A term popularized by Andrew Ng (the host).
  • Definition: the discipline of systematically engineering the data to build a successful AI system.
  • It applies across all dataset sizes:
    • From a small CSV file on your laptop
    • To massive datasets in data warehouses
    • Including how large language models are trained today on trillions of tokens
  • Engineering the data so the AI / machine learning algorithm gets the data it needs to succeed is a wildly exciting, exploding discipline.
  • Data-centric AI will also be covered in this specialization.

What you'll learn

  • Data engineering is an important and specialized skill.
  • The program focuses on frameworks and principles to help you think like a data engineer.
  • It also includes hands-on practical exercises, thanks to a partnership with Amazon Web Services (AWS).
    • You'll build data systems on the cloud.
    • Today, most data engineers build on the cloud.
    • AWS is currently the most widely used public cloud, so AWS skills are a real asset.
  • The courses combine platform-agnostic foundational principles with practical hands-on exercises.

Who this program is for

  • Aspiring data engineers
  • Working data engineers looking to refine their skills
  • People in data-adjacent roles, such as:
    • Data science
    • Machine learning
    • Software engineering
    • Analytics
    • Other data-related roles

Prerequisites

  • Required: prior working experience with data and Python.
  • Nice to have, but not required: some experience with SQL and cloud tools.
  • The course will show you what you need to know and provide suggested resources to learn more.

Program structure: four courses

  • Course 1: The big picture, forming a mental framework for data engineering, and spinning up cloud data pipelines.
  • Course 2: Ingesting data from source systems, DataOps, and orchestration of data pipelines.
  • Course 3: Data storage in the cloud, and how storage is essential to every stage of the data engineering lifecycle.
  • Course 4: Modeling, transforming, and serving data for end use cases.

Wrap-up

  • There's a lot to cover, and Joe is looking forward to sharing it.
  • Next step: move on to the next video and get started.
